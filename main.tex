% ABSTRACT
% =============================================================
\begin{abstract}
Continuous English--Indian Sign Language (ISL) resources have expanded recently, yet parallel annotated corpora remain scarce, and isolated sign lexicons fail to convey sequential discourse. \textcolor{red}{We introduce SignAction, a lightweight modular pipeline translating English text and spoken audio into sequential ISL gestures on standard CPU hardware without GPU acceleration or parallel-corpus training. The architecture decouples into three stages: rule-based NLP normalizing text into uppercase gloss tokens; a multi-tier asset resolver searching 347 indexed sign videos (344 distinct recordings across 36 alphanumeric and 311 lexical signs) via greedy phrase mapping and fingerspelling; and an algorithmic kinematic engine generating 2D stick-figure animations for unresolved vocabulary. An on-device Vosk engine transcribes audio offline. A FastAPI backend serves ordered gesture sequences over REST to a Next.js web client for seamless browser playback. We detail the system architecture, validate execution across 16 automated tests, profile stage workloads qualitatively, and analyze deployment constraints. Importantly, SignAction performs lexical retrieval rather than grammatical ISL translation. Fallback animations offer deterministic placeholders rather than certified signs, leaving syntax reordering, non-manual markers, and asset provenance as clear operational boundaries.}
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Indian Sign Language, sign language translation, speech-to-text, natural language processing, accessibility, gesture synthesis, \textcolor{red}{assistive technology}
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\textcolor{red}{Over five million individuals in India are deaf or hard of hearing according to official census figures, with non-governmental surveys estimating tens of millions \cite{sharma2025}. Globally, disabling hearing loss affected 430 million people in 2021 and is projected to exceed 700 million by 2050 \cite{who2021}. Like all natural sign languages, Indian Sign Language (ISL) exhibits rich grammatical, spatial, and morphological structures \cite{stokoe1960, klima1979}. It serves as the primary medium for education, communication, and civic engagement across India's Deaf community \cite{bragg2019}.}

\textcolor{red}{Despite this substantial demographic need, computational resources for ISL lag far behind those developed for major spoken languages and Western sign benchmarks \cite{joshi2024isign}. Automated end-to-end translation frameworks typically demand extensive parallel video--text corpora \cite{camgoz2020}. While isolated datasets such as INCLUDE \cite{sridhar2020} and CISLR \cite{joshi2022cislr} exist alongside continuous collections like ISLTranslate \cite{joshi2023isltranslate} and iSign \cite{joshi2024isign}, available parallel samples remain limited. Even with the official ISLRTC dictionary documenting 10{,}000 lexical entries \cite{islrtc2021}, continuous sentence data remain insufficient for training reliable generative neural models.}

\textcolor{red}{To navigate these resource constraints, SignAction adopts an offline, rule-driven strategy on commodity CPUs. The pipeline transcribes incoming speech locally using Vosk, cleans and parses text into gloss tokens via linguistic rules, and queries an indexed repository of pre-recorded sign clips. Unmatched words cascade into fingerspelling or algorithmic stick-figure animations. Rather than claiming full grammatical translation or linguistically certified signing, our primary focus is assessing whether a modular, lightweight architecture can deliver immediate lexical visual support without parallel training corpora or dedicated GPUs.}

\textcolor{red}{This paper offers four principal contributions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textcolor{red}{a modular three-tier pipeline isolating NLP, asset lookup, and user presentation;}
    \item \textcolor{red}{a greedy longest-match resolution engine integrating alias normalization and letter fingerspelling;}
    \item \textcolor{red}{a deterministic 24-frame kinematic generator for out-of-vocabulary visual placeholders; and}
    \item \textcolor{red}{an edge-ready implementation combining FastAPI, Next.js 14, Vosk, and Capacitor.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Sign Language Linguistics and Representation}

\textcolor{red}{Foundational linguistic analyses by Stokoe \cite{stokoe1960} and Klima and Bellugi \cite{klima1979} demonstrated that sign languages decompose into discrete phonological parameters: handshape, spatial location, movement trajectory, palm orientation, and facial expression. Cooper et al. \cite{cooper2012} later formalized these manual components into computational sub-units. While gloss annotations offer convenient text tokens for translation pipelines \cite{neidle2001, camgoz2020}, they fail to capture spatial agreement, directional morphology, or non-manual grammatical cues \cite{bragg2019}. In SignAction, uppercase gloss tokens serve strictly as database retrieval handles rather than exhaustive linguistic transcriptions.}

\subsection{Indian Sign Language Computational Resources}

\textcolor{red}{Available ISL computational corpora partition broadly into isolated vocabulary and continuous discourse. For isolated signs, INCLUDE provides 4{,}287 clips across 263 classes \cite{sridhar2020}, whereas CISLR aggregates 4{,}780 videos across 94 categories \cite{joshi2022cislr}. Continuous resources encompass ISLTranslate with roughly 31{,}000 parallel pairs \cite{joshi2023isltranslate}, recent iSign benchmark sets \cite{joshi2024isign}, and character-level fingerspelling data \cite{kirandevraj2025}. Lexical standardization is anchored by the 10{,}000-term ISLRTC dictionary \cite{islrtc2021}, while systems like VibeTalk investigate multimodal dialogue assistance \cite{undri2026vibetalk}. Rather than training deep generative models on these limited samples, our work targets deterministic, offline lexical retrieval on resource-constrained devices.}

\subsection{Gesture Synthesis and Avatar Presentation}

\textcolor{red}{Virtual gesture generation historically relied on 3D geometric avatars, with systems like VSigns driving VRML skeletons via SignWriting notation \cite{papadogiorgaki2004, rastgoo2021}. More recent efforts exploit deep learning, including Text2Sign's GAN-guided motion graphs \cite{stoll2020} and Progressive Transformers for continuous pose sequences \cite{saunders2020}. However, these models demand intensive GPU execution and extensive motion-capture training data. SignAction bypasses heavyweight 3D rendering by serving curated real-world video recordings for known lexicon words, reserving algorithmic 2D stick figures strictly as an edge-rendered fallback.}

\subsection{Offline Speech Recognition}

\textcolor{red}{Modern speech processing often employs large Transformer decoders like Whisper, which achieve exceptional accuracy at the expense of substantial RAM and GPU overhead \cite{radford2023}. Conversely, Vosk packages lightweight Kaldi-based acoustic models capable of real-time offline transcription on low-power CPUs \cite{vosk2021, povey2011}. We select Vosk as the primary audio frontend for edge independence, while preserving modular hooks for optional Whisper integration.}

\section{Working Principle}
\label{sec:method}

\textcolor{red}{Execution advances through four phases: audio/text acquisition, rule-based gloss extraction, multi-tier asset resolution, and browser playback.}

\subsection{Input Acquisition and Audio Normalization}

Text inputs arrive as UTF-8 strings in JSON payloads. For speech input, browser audio typically arrives as WebM blobs. Vosk requires single-channel, 16\,kHz, 16-bit linear PCM audio. The audio pipeline (\texttt{audio.py} and \texttt{routes/speech.py}) performs three processing steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Container Transcoding:} If WebM or non-WAV formats are detected, \texttt{ffmpeg} transcodes the stream to mono WAV.
    \item \textbf{Polyphase Resampling:} \textcolor{red}{Given an arbitrary input rate $f_s$, rational conversion to the required 16\,kHz stream computes integer factors via \eqref{eq:resample}:}
    \begin{equation}
    p = \frac{16000}{\gcd(f_s, 16000)}, \quad q = \frac{f_s}{\gcd(f_s, 16000)}
    \label{eq:resample}
    \end{equation}
    where $p$ and $q$ represent the upsampling and downsampling factors, respectively.
    \item \textbf{PCM-16 Scaling:} \textcolor{red}{Floating-point amplitudes $x \in [-1.0, 1.0]$ are bounded and quantized into 16-bit signed integers through \eqref{eq:pcm}:}
    \begin{equation}
    x_{\text{pcm}} = \text{round}\left( \text{clip}(x, -1.0, 1.0) \times 32767.0 \right)
    \label{eq:pcm}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The resulting buffer is transcribed by Vosk's Kaldi recognizer to produce an English transcript string.

\subsection{Gloss Generation and Lexical Normalization}
\label{subsec:nlp}

The NLP module (\texttt{signaction/nlp.py}) processes raw English text using spaCy's \texttt{en\_core\_web\_sm} pipeline (falling back to a regex-based sentencizer if the model is absent). \textcolor{red}{Text normalizes through four deterministic operations:}

\textbf{(1) Filtering:} Punctuation and whitespace tokens are stripped. Colloquial filler tokens (``um'', ``uh'', ``like'') are dropped when enabled.

\textbf{(2) Pronoun Normalization:} \textcolor{red}{A static 13-entry table converts personal and possessive pronouns into uppercase gloss keys using $g(t)$ from \eqref{eq:pronoun}:}
\begin{equation}
g(t) = 
\begin{cases}
\text{PRONOUN\_MAP}[t] & \text{if } t \in \text{PRONOUN\_MAP} \\
t.\text{upper()} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:pronoun}
\end{equation}
For example, ``i'' and ``me'' map to \texttt{ME}; ``my'' maps to \texttt{MY}; and ``you'' and ``your'' map to \texttt{YOU} and \texttt{YOUR}. \textcolor{red}{Crucially, this step is purely lexical; it does not establish spatial indexing or 3D pronominal pointing.}

\textbf{(3) Lemmatization and Auxiliary Preservation:} Content words are reduced to base forms (e.g., ``walked'' $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WALK}). \textcolor{red}{Conversely, auxiliary verbs (\texttt{AM}, \texttt{IS}, \texttt{ARE}, \texttt{DO}, \texttt{BE}) and continuous participles (\texttt{DOING}, \texttt{EATING}) bypass lemmatization, retaining their surface forms for alias table matching.}

\textbf{(4) Stopword Filtering:} Stopword removal is disabled by default because conversational sign lexicons require question words (\texttt{WHAT}, \texttt{WHERE}) and negations (\texttt{NOT}, \texttt{NO}). When enabled, a protected whitelist prevents dropping grammatical particles.

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{Word-Order Limitation:} Token processing adheres strictly to input English syntax (Subject-Verb-Object). The system executes no tree transformations into ISL's canonical Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) order.}

\textbf{Tier 1 --- Alias and Phrase Mapping:} Tokens are first rewritten using an alias dictionary (158 entries loaded from \texttt{lexicon.json}), standardizing synonyms (e.g., \texttt{HI} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{HELLO}, \texttt{CAN'T} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{CANNOT}). Subsequently, multi-token idioms are extracted via greedy longest-match scanning (\textcolor{red}{outlined in Fig.~\ref{fig:phrase}}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Token sequence $T = [t_1, \ldots, t_n]$, phrase dictionary $P = \{(p_j, r_j)\}$ sorted by phrase token length descending.
\ENSURE Rewritten sequence $T'$
\STATE $T' \gets \emptyset, \quad i \gets 1$
\WHILE{$i \leq n$}
    \STATE $\mathrm{matched} \gets \textsc{False}$
    \FOR{each $(p_j, r_j) \in P$}
        \STATE $m \gets |p_j|$
        \IF{$i + m - 1 \leq n$ \textbf{and} $[t_i, \ldots, t_{i+m-1}] = p_j$}
            \STATE append $r_j$ to $T'$
            \STATE $i \gets i + m$
            \STATE $\mathrm{matched} \gets \textsc{True}$; \textbf{break}
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \IF{$\neg \mathrm{matched}$}
        \STATE append $t_i$ to $T'$; \quad $i \gets i + 1$
    \ENDIF
\ENDWHILE
\RETURN $T'$
\end{algorithmic}
\caption{Greedy longest-match phrase mapping algorithm.}
\label{fig:phrase}
\end{figure}

Because $P$ is sorted descending by token length, longer phrases take precedence over substrings (e.g., ``SEE YOU LATER'' matches before ``SEE YOU''). Overlapping phrases are consumed greedily left-to-right. Default mapped phrases include greetings and courtesy expressions (\texttt{THANK\_YOU}, \texttt{GOOD\_MORNING}, \texttt{HOW\_ARE\_YOU}).

\textbf{Tier 2 --- Filesystem Asset Lookup:} Local media are queried via \texttt{SignLexicon}. File stems are cleaned with \texttt{\_norm\_key()} to enforce case-insensitive matching. \textcolor{red}{To optimize lookup speed, the resolver caches directory trees in memory using filesystem modification timestamps (\texttt{st\_mtime\_ns}). While warm queries read straight from RAM, pipeline executions still verify file paths, refreshing the index if disk contents change.} Search directory priority depends on token length: single alphabetic characters prioritize the \texttt{alphabet/} subdirectory, while multi-character tokens prioritize the \texttt{signs/} subdirectory.

\textbf{Tier 3 --- Fingerspelling Fallback:} \textcolor{red}{When multi-letter words ($|t|>1$) lack whole-sign video recordings, the engine segments them into single-letter characters as shown in \eqref{eq:fingerspell}:}
\begin{equation}
\text{fingerspell}(\text{``PYTHON''}) = [\text{``P''}, \text{``Y''}, \text{``T''}, \text{``H''}, \text{``O''}, \text{``N''}]
\label{eq:fingerspell}
\end{equation}
Each character is resolved against the pre-recorded alphabet sign assets.

\subsection{Kinematic Stick-Figure Fallback Synthesis}
\label{subsec:ai_fallback}

When an isolated token cannot be matched to a pre-recorded video or when a single character asset is missing, \texttt{ai\_fallback.py} algorithmically synthesizes an animated 2D stick figure.

\textbf{Kinematic Skeleton Model:} The upper body is modeled as a 2D kinematic chain centered on a $320 \times 360$ canvas, comprising landmark coordinates for head, neck, shoulders, elbows, and wrists. The non-dominant left arm rests in a neutral pose, \textcolor{red}{while the dominant right wrist follows the time-varying trajectory formulated in \eqref{eq:wrist}:}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}(t) = \mathbf{L}_{\text{target}} + \mathbf{m}(t), \quad t \in [0, N-1]
\label{eq:wrist}
\end{equation}
where $N = 24$ \textcolor{red}{frames (displayed at 60\,ms per frame, yielding a 1.44\,s motion loop)}, $\mathbf{L}_{\text{target}}$ denotes a target body landmark (Forehead, Chin, Cheek, Chest, or Neutral signing space), and $\mathbf{m}(t) = [A_x \sin(\omega_x t),\, A_y \cos(\omega_y t)]^T$ defines periodic wrist displacement. Handshapes are drawn via geometric finger strokes (flat hand, fist, pointing index, V-shape, hook, pinch).

\textbf{Handcrafted Parameters vs. Hash Mapping:} \textcolor{red}{For unrecorded words, the kinematic module maintains 228 handcrafted token keys (entirely separate from the 311 video assets) mapping to 42 parametric sign definitions derived from ISLRTC dictionary footage. When inputs fall outside this internal vocabulary, the system assigns an index $f \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ using \eqref{eq:hash}:}
\begin{equation}
f = \text{MD5}(\text{token}) \bmod 4
\label{eq:hash}
\end{equation}
mapping the unknown token to one of four generic motion families: (0) bilateral neutral wave, (1) chest-level arc, (2) vertical forehead tap, or (3) forward neutral sweep.

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{Non-Semantic Status of Fallback Gestures:} Hash-selected trajectories ($f \in \{0,1,2,3\}$) act strictly as \emph{visual placeholders}. While mathematically repeatable, they convey no certified linguistic or lexical meaning in ISL.}

\subsection{\textcolor{red}{Evaluation Methodology and Source-Wise Analysis}}
\label{subsec:eval_status}

\textcolor{red}{Importantly, automated tests confirm software reliability rather than linguistic fidelity or native signer comprehension. Because the project repository contains no standardized evaluation corpus, reference alignments, or formal scoring logs, we claim no automated translation scores (such as BLEU or ROUGE) or empirical accuracy percentages. Rather than presenting unsupported quantitative ratings, we examine qualitative pipeline behavior across three distinct linguistic genres explored during pilot trials:}

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{(1) Conversational Interaction:} Inputs include standard daily greetings, queries, and courtesy remarks (e.g., ``HELLO'', ``HOW ARE YOU'', ``THANK YOU''). Because these phrases align directly with the curated alias tables and 311 lexical sign recordings, they resolve smoothly to pre-recorded clips without letter decomposition. However, question structures lack essential facial grammar (such as brow furrowing for wh-interrogatives), and pronouns map at the word level without spatial loci.}

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{(2) Descriptive Scene Captions (COCO Style):} These sentences detail physical environments, concrete objects, and spatial arrangements. Lexical nouns and action verbs resolve reliably to local video assets. In contrast, unmapped compound phrases (such as ``traffic light'') and fine-grained modifiers miss Tier~2, triggering letter-by-letter fingerspelling. Furthermore, spatial prepositions are rendered as isolated sequential signs rather than establishing spatial coordinates in 3D signing space.}

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{(3) Formal News and Media Excerpts:} Characterized by complex syntax, passive voice, temporal clauses, and specialized domain vocabulary, these inputs present the greatest challenge. Unrecorded technical terms and named entities cascade into sequential fingerspelling or hash-selected stick-figure placeholders. Most critically, because SignAction processes tokens in English Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order, embedded clauses appear word-for-word without structural conversion to ISL's native Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) syntax.}

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{Evaluation Gap and Benchmarking Requirements:} Rigorous empirical validation requires an open, standardized ISL benchmark spanning conversational, descriptive, and formal registers, paired with expert gloss annotations and Deaf-community comprehensibility evaluations.}

\subsection{Deployment Footprint}

\textcolor{red}{The backend service packages into a lightweight container image (\texttt{python:3.11-slim}) bundling FastAPI, the compact spaCy English pipeline, and the offline Vosk speech recognition model without external database infrastructure. % 6. LIMITATIONS

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textcolor{red}{Several technical, linguistic, and operational boundaries characterize the current implementation:}

\textbf{(1) English-Oriented Word Order:} Tokens are parsed sequentially in standard English Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order. In contrast, Indian Sign Language relies on Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) structures, topic-comment arrangements, and time-first temporal adverbials. The resulting visual stream thus reflects manually coded English rather than authentic ISL grammar.

\textbf{(2) Context and Ambiguity:} The glosser operates on isolated word tokens without semantic context windows. Polysemous words and homonyms (such as ``bank'' denoting a financial firm versus a riverbank) cannot be disambiguated, risking incorrect asset retrieval in ambiguous sentences.

\textbf{(3) Lexical Vocabulary Coverage:} \textcolor{red}{The local repository remains bounded at 311 word-level sign clips (spanning 308 lexical concepts) and 36 alphanumeric recordings. While Tier~3 decomposes unknown words into alphabet fingerspelling, fingerspelling extended terminology proves visually taxing and far slower than native holistic signs.}

\textbf{(4) Absence of Non-Manual Markers:} Natural ISL relies on non-manual signals---such as eyebrow furrowing for wh-questions, brow raises for polar queries, head tilts, mouthing, and body shifts---to encode grammatical mood, polarity, and clause boundaries. Both the prerecorded video library and stick-figure animations depict manual hand gestures alone, omitting vital facial grammar.

\textbf{(5) Single-View 2D Representation:} The algorithmic kinematic fallback renders exclusively on a 2D planar canvas from an unvarying frontal view. Natural signing exploits a 3D volume around the signer's torso to establish referential loci and directional verb morphology, which a planar projection cannot represent.

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{(6) Asset Licensing and Provenance Constraints:} Although the software pipeline and fallback generators are openly inspectable, the 347 sign video clips were collated from public educational repositories without an itemized provenance ledger. Their legal redistribution rights remain unverified, mandating replacement with community-licensed native recordings prior to production deployment.}

\textcolor{red}{\textbf{(7) Absence of Native Signer Comprehensibility Studies:} Although software functionality and computational profiles have been verified, the pipeline has not yet been subjected to formal user trials or comprehensibility ratings with Deaf native ISL signers.}

% =============================================================
% 7. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
% =============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclude}

\textcolor{red}{SignAction demonstrates that an edge-friendly, modular pipeline can supply sequential lexical sign support on commodity CPUs without requiring GPUs or parallel training corpora. By combining rule-based NLP, cached video retrieval, alphabet fingerspelling, and kinematic fallback synthesis, the system offers an offline-capable assistive prototype. Subsequent iterations will focus on SOV syntactic reordering, contextual word sense disambiguation, native-signer community-cleared recordings, non-manual facial cues via expressive avatars, and formal comprehensibility trials conducted with India's Deaf community.}
